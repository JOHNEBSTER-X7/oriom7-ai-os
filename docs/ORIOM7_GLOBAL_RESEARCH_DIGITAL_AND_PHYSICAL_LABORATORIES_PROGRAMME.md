# ORIOM7 Global Research, Digital & Physical Laboratories Programme

**Status:** Proposed programme baseline  
**Date:** 13 November 2026  
**Programme owner:** ORIOM7  
**Related programme:** Global Technology Intelligence & Proprietary Product Development

## 1. Purpose

Establish an integrated network of digital laboratories, physical laboratories, research facilities, testing centres and commercialisation capabilities. The laboratories will support continuous discovery, research, invention, prototyping, verification and commercial development across technology and other selected industries.

The programme extends the existing ORIOM7 AI Search Engine & AI Operating System. It does not change the approved initial delivery priorities: search, assistant and developer API. Laboratory capabilities are introduced in stages and must not be represented as operational until built, staffed, validated and authorised.

## 2. Operating principles

- Digital research and simulation should precede costly physical experiments where scientifically appropriate.
- Physical testing must validate claims that cannot be established through simulation alone.
- Use shared infrastructure and specialist facilities rather than duplicating expensive equipment.
- Partner with universities, accredited laboratories, foundries, contract manufacturers and specialist providers before committing to capital-intensive facilities.
- Maintain safety, environmental, privacy, security, export-control and regulatory controls appropriate to each discipline.
- Use qualified scientists and engineers for decisions requiring professional judgement.
- AI may assist research but must not fabricate experimental results, citations, test data or claims of invention.
- Preserve reproducibility through versioned designs, datasets, experiment protocols, equipment calibration records and signed result provenance.
- Keep research hypotheses, prototypes, validated products and released products clearly distinguished.

## 6. ORIOM7 laboratory data architecture

Core data domains:

- Organisations, facilities, rooms and access zones.
- Users, research teams, qualifications and authorisations.
- Equipment, assets, sensors, calibration and maintenance.
- Projects, work packages, milestones and budgets.
- Research proposals, hypotheses and invention disclosures.
- Protocols, experiment runs and parameter sets.
- Samples, materials and chain-of-custody events.
- Datasets, model versions, simulation jobs and results.
- Observations, measurements, uncertainty and analysis.
- Safety assessments, incidents and approvals.
- IP records, agreements, licences and disclosures.
- Product requirements, designs, bills of materials and revisions.
- Suppliers, purchase orders and inventory.
- Quality records, verification and release decisions.

Every experiment should capture, where applicable:
- Unique experiment and project identifiers.
- Research question and hypothesis.
- Protocol and protocol version.
- Researcher and reviewer.
- Equipment identifiers and calibration state.
- Input materials, sample IDs and provenance.
- Dataset, software, model and design versions.
- Environmental and operating conditions.
- Raw data location and cryptographic integrity metadata.
- Analysis method and result.
- Uncertainty, limitations and deviations.
- Safety authorisation and approval.
- Timestamped audit trail.

Do not overwrite raw measurements. Corrections should be appended with authorship, reason and review history.

## 7. Research-to-invention workflow

1. Discover a problem, unmet need or scientific opportunity.
2. Gather lawful, relevant and traceable evidence.
3. Review prior art, standards and existing solutions.
4. Form a testable research hypothesis.
5. Define requirements, risks and success criteria.
6. Select digital simulation, bench testing or both.
7. Design and version the experiment.
8. Obtain safety and ethics approvals where required.
9. Run the experiment under qualified supervision.
10. Record raw observations and uncertainty.
11. Independently review and reproduce material results.
12. Evaluate technical feasibility and differentiation.
13. Conduct IP ownership and freedom-to-operate review.
14. Build a prototype.
15. Test usability, security, reliability and performance.
16. Validate customer need and unit economics.
17. Decide whether to license, manufacture, partner, invest further or stop.
18. Monitor the product after release and feed learning back into research.

## 8. Example: ORIOM7 accelerator research pathway

1. Use ORIOM7 Registry to collect sourced information on public GPU and AI accelerator specifications.
2. Characterise actual ORIOM7 model inference and search workloads.
3. Identify measurable bottlenecks in memory, interconnect, compute, power or software.
4. Define an original architecture hypothesis.
5. Review academic prior art, patents, standards and licensing.
6. Model the architecture and performance assumptions.
7. Develop and verify RTL where feasible.
8. Prototype selected functions on FPGA.
9. Benchmark against comparable systems using reproducible workloads.
10. Assess power, performance, area, cost and manufacturability.
11. Obtain independent design and IP review.
12. Seek foundry and packaging partners if justified.
13. Commission a test chip only after technical and investment approval.
14. Validate silicon and decide whether to commercialise.

Simulation estimates must be labelled as estimates; FPGA results must not be represented as ASIC performance; and no product superiority claim should be made without independent, comparable evidence.

## 9. Governance and safety

Establish:
- Laboratory director and discipline leads.
- Research ethics and integrity review.
- Health, safety and environmental management.
- Equipment qualification and calibration.
- Data governance and privacy controls.
- Cybersecurity and physical access controls.
- IP and legal review.
- Quality assurance and document control.
- Independent technical review.
- Incident response and business continuity.

For UK operations, determine the applicable HSE, COSHH, environmental, electrical, radiation, biological containment, medical-device and other sector-specific requirements before facility design or commissioning. Requirements depend on the actual activity and facility.

## 10. Delivery phases

### Phase 0 — Programme definition
- Select initial research domains.
- Identify users, research partners and target customers.
- Define governance, IP, safety and data policies.
- Map available equipment and external facilities.
- Establish indicative capital and operating budgets.

### Phase 1 — Digital laboratory MVP
- Build ORIOM7 LAB OS research projects and experiment registry.
- Integrate literature, patent and technology discovery.
- Add research notebooks and provenance.
- Add model, dataset and simulation job tracking.
- Connect to existing ORIOM7 search, knowledge graph and API.

### Phase 2 — Shared digital infrastructure
- Provide managed GPU/CPU compute access.
- Integrate CAD, EDA and simulation tools.
- Add secure data storage and project workspaces.
- Introduce reproducible benchmarking and evaluation.

### Phase 3 — Partner-operated physical research
- Contract university and specialist laboratory access.
- Use external electronics, FPGA, materials and testing facilities.
- Establish equipment booking, sample tracking and result ingestion.
- Validate the research-to-prototype workflow.

### Phase 4 — Initial owned physical laboratory
- Select one or two high-value facilities based on demand.
- Complete feasibility, site, safety and equipment studies.
- Procure and qualify equipment.
- Recruit trained personnel.
- Commission, validate and audit before operation.

### Phase 5 — Specialist laboratory expansion
- Add semiconductor, robotics, energy, materials and other facilities based on validated project demand.
- Establish specialist partnerships and regional centres.
- Introduce additional quality and accreditation systems where commercially or legally required.

### Phase 6 — Global research network
- Connect approved university, industrial and contract research partners.
- Share non-confidential research outputs under agreed licences.
- Establish joint IP and commercialisation agreements.
- Build regional innovation and manufacturing partnerships.

## 11. Initial success measures

- Research time from question to evidence-backed proposal.
- Percentage of experiments with complete provenance.
- Reproducibility rate of selected experiments.
- Simulation-to-measurement error.
- Prototype success rate.
- Equipment utilisation and downtime.
- Cost per completed experiment.
- Research-to-patent assessment conversion.
- Invention-to-customer-validation conversion.
- Product development cycle time.
- Number of validated commercial products.
- Revenue and licensing income from released products.
- Safety incidents and corrective action closure.

Do not optimise for the number of patents or inventions alone; measure reproducibility, customer value and defensible technical differentiation.
